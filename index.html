What I work on

I work on AI that can understand and create all kinds of audio, not just speech. That covers a few connected areas: speech and language processing, representation learning (letting models pick up words, speakers, emotion, music and everyday sounds on their own), neural audio codecs that squeeze audio into compact tokens a model can work with, and speech language models that understand and generate speech directly, the way text models handle text.

I'm also interested in multimodal learning, especially how pairing speech with other modalities can make systems better at both.

The long-term goal is one general-purpose audio foundation model: something that can understand and generate speech, music and environmental sound, sound natural, take direction from the person using it, and still run without a data centre behind it.

  • Aug – Oct 2025Remote

    Summer of AI Researcher

    EleutherAI

    Multimodal LLMs sometimes "hear" things that aren't there because noisy acoustic detail throws them off. I tackled this by combining SemantiCodec's low-bitrate semantic tokens with acoustic tokens in a dual-encoder setup, tested on AVHBench. It cut hallucinations by 1.5% compared with the baseline.

  • Sep 2024 – presentRemote

    Independent Researcher

    ML Collective

    I fine-tuned the BGE-M3 embedding model to search documents in Yorùbá, Igbo and Hausa, and improved Mean Reciprocal Rank by 20% over the original model. The paper appeared at the Widening NLP workshop, and the poster won a Poster Award at Deep Learning Indaba 2025.

  • Mar 2022 – Sep 2024Abeokuta, Nigeria

    Undergraduate Researcher

    Communication and Signal Processing Lab, FUNAAB

    More than 40 million people speak Yorùbá, yet few AI tools can talk with them in it. So I built YorubaAI, a speech-to-speech assistant that listens, finds relevant Yorùbá content and answers out loud. I fine-tuned Whisper-small on Yorùbá Common Voice (13.06% word error rate), grounded the answers with LaBSE retrieval and deployed a demo on Hugging Face Spaces.
